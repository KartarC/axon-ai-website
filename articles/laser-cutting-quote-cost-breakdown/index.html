Quoting guide

What belongs in a laser-cutting quote?

By Ovrendi · October 2, 2026

A laser-cutting estimate should account for material, machine time, assist gas, electricity, setup and any additional operations. Review quantities and units first, then apply a consistent pricing policy without counting an expense twice.

In this guideEstablish the production basisUse the shop’s actual rate assumptionsKeep gas and electricity units consistentReview the commercial result

Establish the production basis

Record the material grade, thickness, sheet size, number of sheets, part quantities and drawing revision. Confirm whether a nesting report covers one sheet, one run or the whole job. A time or cost copied from a single-sheet report must not be mistaken for a complete batch total. Preserve the source report alongside the estimate.

Use the shop’s actual rate assumptions

For full-sheet pricing, the material starting point is sheet count multiplied by price per sheet, adjusted only by a deliberate remnant or customer-supplied-material policy. Machine cost uses the agreed chargeable time and hourly rate. Setup, handling, deburring and outside services need their own treatment if they are not already included.

Keep gas and electricity units consistent

Gas estimates require a consumption basis and a matching purchase-cost basis. A bottle-pack price alone is insufficient without usable gas volume and any stated loss allowance. Electricity estimates use electrical input power in kW, time in hours and a price per kWh. The laser’s optical power rating is not the same as the total electrical input of the machine and auxiliaries. Use documented machine or shop measurements instead of invented defaults.

Review the commercial result

Markup and margin produce different selling prices: a 20% markup on a hypothetical $100 cost gives $120, while a 20% gross margin gives $125. These examples are arithmetic illustrations, not recommended rates. State currency, quantity, tax treatment, quote validity and exclusions in the customer document. Keep internal rates and margin out of that document.

A checklist to take back to your team

  • Does the source report match the requested batch?
  • Are gas volume and flow units compatible?
  • Is electricity already included in the machine rate?
  • Are setup and finishing operations covered?
  • Has a person reviewed the final amount before issue?
