Production & job tracking

Learn the production workflow when this module is enabled for your company.

Updated October 18, 2026 · Based on the current Ovrendi workflow

In this tutorial

Open Quoting

Check access

Production Board must be enabled for your company. A Quoting-only trial does not include it. Owners, admins and managers can convert quotes and assign work. Operators can update stages, instructions and usage. Viewers can read job progress.

Turn an accepted quote into a job

  1. Open the saved quotation in Quoting and select Review.
  2. Issue it and collect customer acceptance through the private approval link, or record acceptance after receiving it separately.
  3. Refresh responses, then select Create / open production job.

The accepted revision supplies the customer name, material, part dimensions, quantities and quoted price. Repeating the action opens the same job. One accepted quote batch creates one job. A matching customer record is linked only when its name is unambiguous.

Plan the work

Open a job card. Assign a machine, due date and priority. Add drawing references, secondary operations and inspection instructions. Machine choices come from your company’s machine list; quoting cost profiles are separate.

For work without a quotation, use Operation board / new job. These jobs also appear in job tracking.

Track the batch

Save the job’s stage as work progresses: Planned → Cutting → Secondary operations → Inspection → Ready for pickup → Completed. Use On hold or Cancelled when needed. Completed represents fulfilled work. Stages are staff-recorded; they do not certify inspection or automatically check traveler sign-offs.

The operation board tracks individual operations separately. Changing a job stage does not automatically complete its operation cards. Use Shop Travelers, if enabled, for existing instructions and sign-off workflows.

Employee timers and timesheets

  1. Sign in with your own account and open a production job.
  2. Save pending edits, then choose Start my timer. Your timer continues through refreshes and closing the browser. Each employee can run one personal timer at a time, and several employees can work on the same job.
  3. Choose Stop my timer when you pause work. Your completed session is saved to your timesheet and added to the job’s cumulative person-hours.
  4. Managers open Employee timesheets from Production to filter by date, employee/job search and review status, stop a forgotten timer, and mark completed sessions reviewed with an optional note.

Employees see their own records; owners, admins and managers see the company’s records. Timesheets show up to 500 sessions, with running timers first; narrow dates for older records. Date filters use the session start date in UTC; displayed timestamps use your device timezone.

Automatic labour cost uses the job’s saved hourly rate on the total recorded hours. For example, two people each working one hour add two person-hours. Manual labour overrides remain fixed. Review does not add costs a second time. Individual employee pay rates, payroll processing and approval-gated costing are not part of this workflow.

Use personal timers or the shared timer, never both simultaneously on the same job. Stop all employee timers before completing or pausing a job. Recorded sessions remain an audit trail; corrections to the job’s cumulative hours are separate from timesheet review.

Use the shared job timer

  1. Open a job and save any pending edits.
  2. Select Start timer. One shared timer runs for the whole job; it continues through refreshes, device changes and closing the browser.
  3. Select Stop timer when work pauses. Elapsed time is added to the existing actual hours and automatic labour cost is recalculated using the job’s saved rate.

Job Costing includes this session after you stop it. Manual labour totals stay fixed. Stop the timer before adjusting total hours, putting the job on hold, or marking it ready, completed or cancelled. Managers and operators can start or stop the shared timer. This tracks elapsed job time, not separate person-hours for multiple workers.

For a forgotten timer, stop it, correct the cumulative hours, and save. Timer start and stop events remain in the activity history.

Record hours and costs

  1. Enter cumulative actual production hours for the whole job.
  2. Choose Automatic: hours × saved rate. By default, the saved labour rate from the accepted quotation is used. You can select one of your company’s saved Quoting profiles instead.
  3. Save progress. Labour cost is actual hours multiplied by the saved hourly labour rate, rounded to two decimals. The profile currency must match the accepted quotation. Machine hourly costs are separate and are not used as labour rates.
  4. For an adjustment, choose Manual total, enter the total and a reason, and save. Further hours changes will keep that total. Switch back to Automatic to recalculate.

The chosen rate is copied to the job. Editing a profile later does not change existing jobs; select that profile again to apply its latest rate. Missing hours or rates leave the automatic total unrecorded. Previously entered manual totals are preserved.

Enter actual material cost separately. Job Costing uses the saved production totals without adding duplicate labour or material charges. Outside services and overhead remain additional costs. The accepted quotation’s price is preserved for comparison. Inventory is not deducted.

Manage reusable hourly rates in Quoting’s saved machine profiles. Only owners, admins and managers can select rates or adjust costs. Operators can update hours and automatic totals will recalculate securely.

Save and review

Select Save job progress. Changes and stage history are saved to your company account. If another user updated the job first, reload the saved job and reapply your changes. Use search, machine and stage filters to find work. Refresh the board for the latest changes. The board shows up to 500 recent jobs; history shows the latest 50 updates per job.
